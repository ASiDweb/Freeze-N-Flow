State Raoult’s law and use it to calculate the vapour-pressure lowering of a solution. Calculate the vapour composition above a solution containing a volatile solute and solvent.

Live Explanation

According to Raoult’s law, the partial vapour pressure of a component in an ideal solution is equal to its mole fraction multiplied by its vapour pressure in the pure state.

For a non-volatile solute, the solute reduces the mole fraction of the solvent.

Therefore, the vapour pressure of the solution is lower than that of the pure solvent. For a solution containing volatile components, the total vapour pressure is obtained by adding the partial vapour pressures of the components.

Experiment Summary

Observation

Glucose lowers the vapour pressure of water. Ethanol is volatile and contributes its own vapour, so the mixture may produce more vapour than pure water.

Real-life Application

A wet towel soaked in salty sea water takes longer to dry in the sun than a towel soaked in pure tap water, because the salt blocks the water from evaporating quickly.

Live Explanation

A liquid boils when its vapour pressure becomes equal to the external pressure.

A non-volatile solute such as glucose lowers the solvent vapour pressure, so a higher temperature is needed.

Ethanol is volatile and adds its own vapour pressure. Therefore, an ethanol–water mixture may boil at a lower temperature than pure water.

Experiment Summary

Observation

A glucose solution boils above pure water, while an ethanol–water mixture may boil below 100 °C because ethanol is volatile.

Real-life Application

Special liquids (coolants) are added to car water tanks. This raises the boiling point so the water inside the hot engine doesn't boil away easily.

Live Explanation

Freezing occurs when the liquid and solid phases of a solvent are in equilibrium.

Dissolved solute particles lower the escaping tendency of solvent molecules in the liquid phase.

Therefore, the solution must be cooled below the freezing point of the pure solvent before the solid solvent can form. This decrease is called freezing-point depression.

Experiment Summary

Observation

Pure water freezes exactly at 0 °C and forms a neat grid. The mixture stays liquid at 0 °C and only freezes when it gets even colder.

Real-life Application

People throw salt on snowy icy roads in winter. The salt mixes with ice and lowers the freezing point, causing dangerous ice to melt into water.

Live Explanation

Osmosis is the net movement of solvent molecules through a semipermeable membrane from a region of lower solute concentration to a region of higher solute concentration.

The membrane allows solvent molecules to pass but prevents solute particles from crossing.

The net movement continues until the concentration difference is balanced by the pressure difference across the membrane.

Experiment Summary

Observation

Water molecules move both ways, but more water gets trapped on the mixture side. The water level on the mixture side goes up.

Real-life Application

Plant roots soak up water from the soil using osmosis. If you put too much fertilizer (solid particles) in the soil, the plant cannot pull water in, and might die.
